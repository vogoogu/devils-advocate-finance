AsterFlow sells maintenance software to factories. It requests €3.0m for 20% of the company after investment. All proceeds go to the company. It proposes €1.2m for product and integrations, €1.2m for overseas sales and €0.6m for working capital.

A3 Customers
The company has 29 paying customers. Its two largest account for 46% of revenue. Eight additional factories are running unpaid pilots. Management expects seven pilots to become paying customers within six months. None has signed a subscription contract. Source: customer register and pilot update.

A4 Growth forecast
Management forecasts €3.0m revenue for the next 12 months. This assumes seven pilot conversions and entry into two new countries, where no sales staff have yet been hired. Planned recruitment will increase monthly spending. No downside cash forecast is supplied. Source: founder presentation.

A5 Product evidence
Six volunteer customers report a median 11% reduction in machine downtime after adoption. There is no comparison group. Three references chosen by the founder are positive. Support records also show three integrations taking twice as long as planned. Source: customer survey, reference calls and support log.

A6 Founder and market
The founder previously led a product team at a software company that was later acquired. A broker email highlights a competitor acquisition, but gives no purchase price or financial figures. The founder describes AsterFlow as "the next category leader". Source: founder biography and broker email.

A7 Terms and timing
The offer is ordinary equity with quarterly reporting and no guaranteed return or exit. The founder requests an answer within 14 days, citing discussions with another investor. Westbridge has not verified that interest. A different amount or stake would require negotiation. Source: indicative offer.
